The problem

Trips abroad make splitting bills messy. One person pays in yen. Another pays in cash. Everyone used a different rate. Someone has to do the maths later.

SplitPea keeps every expense, every rate and every payment in one place, and tells you who pays whom.

Features

groups.png

Groups for any trip

Make a group for a trip abroad (like Japan in ¥) or an everyday group in S$ only.

new-expense.png

Live or your own rate

Add expenses in the trip currency. See the ≈ S$ amount using today's rate, or use your own cash rate. Each expense keeps its rate, so later changes never touch past expenses.

split.png

Split your way

Split equally, or choose who's in.

Scan a receipt

Take a receipt photo. The app reads it on your phone. Tap who had each item. Service charge and GST are shared in proportion to what each person had.

balances.png

Clear balances

Balances show in one main currency (S$), with suggested payments. They are person to person by default. Turn on "Simplify payments" to use fewer payments and see how it got there.

settle.png

Record payments

Record a payment in S$ or ¥ cash, converted at the receiver's rate. Made a mistake? Tap Undo.

Light and dark mode

Green is the main colour. Orange is the accent.

How it works

Create a group

Pick the people and the currency. Japan in ¥? Dinner with friends in S$? Both work.

Add expenses or scan a receipt

Type an amount, or snap the receipt and tap who had what.

Settle up

See who pays whom. Record the payment when it happens.

Under the hood

I designed 31 screens in light and dark mode before writing code. Then I built them in Swift.

Built with

  • Swift and UIKit
  • Programmatic UI (no storyboards)
  • VIPER architecture
  • iOS 18 and up

Apple frameworks

  • Vision: reads receipt text on the device. Photos are not uploaded.
  • PhotosUI: the photo picker. The app never sees your whole photo library.

Web API

  • Frankfurter: daily exchange rates from the European Central Bank.

Money, done carefully

  • Money is stored as whole cents to avoid rounding errors.
  • Leftover cents go to the payer.
